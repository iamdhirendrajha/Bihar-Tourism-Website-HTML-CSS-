Gurpa Hills / Gurupada Giri is one of the natural and historical places located in the Gaya district of Bihar. It is situated on the Bihar-Jharkhand border at a distance of 50 km.

Here Mahakasyapa, the last disciple of Buddha, attained Nirvana. Gurupada Giri is now known as 'Gurpa Hill'. Another name for this hill is 'Kukkutpada Giri'. Natural scenery, forests, waterfalls, sunrise and sunset views all have put Gurpa Hill in great vogue.

There is a temple named Gurupada on Gurpa Hill which is assumed to have footprints of “Lord Vishnu”. This place has gained popularity among the pilgrims of Buddhism and Hinduism. From the top of the Gurpa peak, one can get a breathtaking view of the surrounding countryside. It is an ideal place for meditation. Tourists visit this place throughout the year.

Maya Sarovar is a sacred lake located in Bihar, known for its religious and historical significance. It is believed to be associated with Lord Buddha’s journey and attracts pilgrims from all over the world. The lake is named after Queen Maya, the mother of Gautama Buddha. According to Buddhist traditions, sacred ponds like this played an important role in Buddha’s early life. Today, it remains an important pilgrimage site for Buddhists and travelers.

The scenic beauty of Maya Sarovar adds to its charm. The lake's crystal-clear water reflects the sky, surrounded by lush greenery, making it a peaceful retreat for visitors. The area also holds ancient Buddhist relics and monasteries, drawing history and culture enthusiasts. The tranquil environment makes it a popular spot for meditation, with many monks and spiritual seekers visiting throughout the year.

Tourists visiting Maya Sarovar can experience a spiritual retreat, as many monks and devotees meditate in the serene surroundings. The lake also offers a great opportunity for photography, with its natural beauty providing a perfect backdrop. Nature walks around the lake allow visitors to soak in the peaceful environment, while nearby monasteries provide insight into Buddhist culture and history.

Reaching Maya Sarovar is convenient as it is well connected by various transport options. The nearest airport is Gaya International Airport, located about 80 km away. Travelers can also reach Maya Sarovar by train, with Gaya Railway Station being the closest rail hub. Additionally, the lake is accessible by road, with well-connected highways allowing easy travel by taxis and buses.

Sher Shah Suri, originally named Farid Khan, was born in Sasaram, Bihar, in 1486. His father, Hasan Khan, was a Jagirdar under the rule of the Lodhi dynasty. However, Sher Shah did not inherit a powerful kingdom; instead, he built his legacy through courage, intelligence, and visionary leadership. His early life in Bihar played a crucial role in shaping his personality, where he gained experience in administration and warfare. His bravery in killing a tiger earned him the title "Sher Khan", which later became Sher Shah Suri after he established his rule.

Sher Shah Suri’s Rise to Power

Sher Shah Suri began his career as a local administrator in Bihar, where he displayed exceptional administrative and military skills. Under the service of Bahar Khan Lohani, the ruler of South Bihar, Sher Shah gradually rose to prominence. By 1530, he became the ruler of Bihar and later expanded his influence to Bengal. His biggest achievement came in 1539, when he defeated the Mughal Emperor Humayun in the Battle of Chausa. A year later, in 1540, he again defeated Humayun in the Battle of Kannauj, forcing him into exile and establishing the Sur Empire, which ruled over North India.

Contributions to Bihar

Sher Shah Suri transformed Bihar into a significant administrative, cultural, and trade hub. His policies and reforms in Bihar laid the foundation for modern governance. Some of his major contributions to Bihar include:

1. Administrative Reforms

Sher Shah Suri was known for his efficient and just administration. He divided his empire into Sarkars (districts), each managed by a governor responsible for law and order. His governance style was later adopted by Mughal Emperor Akbar.

2. Revenue System

One of Sher Shah's most remarkable achievements was the introduction of an efficient land revenue system. He conducted a proper land survey and classified land based on fertility, ensuring fair taxation. Farmers in Bihar benefited from these reforms as they were no longer subjected to excessive taxes.
